Typically before an Appointing Party  has decided on the brief, it is likely that they will have to:Justify the project.Appoint advisers.Assess the feasibility of the project.Carry out options studies and select the preferred option.These stages involve sequential high-level decisions. However, typically they are not listed as project stages in standard industry project plans, which tend not to recognise the importance of early employer processes.
The Appointing Party should define their decisions in the way that suits their needs, rather than adhering to industry standards that may have been developed for a particular discipline or procurement route.
## BIM Information Delivery Workstages

The first two stages have been broken down into sub-sections where important employer decisions have been identified that are not included within the RIBA plan of work.
The end of each stage is marked by an employer’s decision point, or gateway, at which the employer needs specific information to help them decide whether the project has developed satisfactorily and whether it should continue to the next stage.
Strategic Definition
'Business justification' is the first part of the 'Strategic definition' stage. It involves making crucial decisions about whether to proceed with the proposed project. the employer identifies a need that could require the development of a newly built asset.
They then prepare a statement of need that describes the requirements for the potential project. This will later develop into a strategic brief.
The employer then prepares a preliminary business case based upon the statement of need. This offers a justification for the investment required by the potential project.
PIR question for this stage - Does the preliminary business case give sufficient justification for the project?
During Strategic Brief – a team is appointed. The employer's internal team develop the information in the statement of need and preliminary business case into a strategic brief that describes in more detail the requirements the built asset will have to satisfy. The strategic brief should be as detailed as possible and should provide sufficient information to enable the appointment of advisers. Key stakeholders should be involved in preparing the strategic brief, including, where possible, the operators of the building.
PIR Question for this stage - Has the strategic brief been developed sufficiently to appoint advisers to carry out feasibility studies and option appraisals?
Appraisals is the first part of the 'preparation and brief' stage. It involves assessing whether the project is feasible, and whether there is a preferred option that should be developed. The advisers or consultants undertake feasibility studies to establish whether the project is viable.
PIR Question for this stage - Is the project feasible?
Do option studies provide sufficient information to be able to select a preferred option?
Information Requirements is the second part of brief stage - It involves preparing info requirement documentation for the appointment of suppliers (Lead & Appointed Parties) PROJECT Brief, EIR, BIM protocol etc
PIR Question for this stage  - Are the employer's information requirements developed sufficiently to make appointments?
Supplier Appointments  take place based upon information requirements and the scope of the brief. Capability and capacity as well as an understanding of the info requirements should be demonstrated.
PIR Question for this stage  -  Do the proposals sufficiently answer the information requirements? Is the capability of the suppliers suitable or is training required?
