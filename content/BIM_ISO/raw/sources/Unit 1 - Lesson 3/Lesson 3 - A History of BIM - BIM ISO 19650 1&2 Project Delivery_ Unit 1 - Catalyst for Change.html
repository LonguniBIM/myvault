Government Construction Strategy

BIM Level 2

The 2011 Government Construction Strategy introduced a fully collaborative BIM process, placing a requirement that by 2016 on all government funded projects BIM level 2 must be adhered to.

BIM level 2 defined what, when and how information should be created, shared and managed.

This meant fully federated models and the exchange of project and asset information through a common data environment (CDE).

BIM Level 2

At the core of PAS 1192:2 is BS 1192.The principles defined in BS1192:2007 were expanded upon for the BIM Level 2 series of documents.

A new suite of documents and standards were created, and as such PAS/BS1192 Parts 2 – 5 were mandated on all public sector projects.

PAS stands for Publicly Available Specification.

The following were also introduced as part of the BIM Level 2 plan:


  • Construction Industry Council (CIC) BIM Protocol (Second Edition) 

The protocol can be appended to any contract form

  • Government Soft Landings : A Government initiative to use BIM  in relation to buildings or assets to gain better value throughout operational lifecycle of an asset.

  • Classification: A classification system provides a common language across the supply chain for naming physical elements, systems and activities. Uniclass 2015 is a UK compliant implementation of ISO 12006-2. 

Other breakdown systems were also used such as CESSM4 Civil Engineering Standard Method of Measurement RICS New Rules of Measurement – NRM 1 

  • Digital Plan of Work – such as the NBS BIM Toolkit

  • PAS 1192-6:2018 was then later added to the suite. Specification for collaborative sharing and use of structured Health and Safety information using BIM.

Government Soft Landings

Brought in along with the UK BIM Level 2 Mandate, BS 8536-1:2015 describes Government Soft Landings (GSL) as:

"A process for the graduated handover of a new or refurbished asset/facility, where a defined period of aftercare by the design and construction team is an owner’s requirement that is planned and developed from the outset of the project from "

Soft landings help to break down the barriers between capital delivery and asset operations.

The image below shows the Government Soft Landings activities for each stage of the project.

A key factor of the Soft Landings process is maintaining a “Golden Thread” of design intent and purpose from the start through to the operation of the asset.

The diagram demonstrates GSL at the inception of a project, through all stages and ending with evaluating the performance of the asset during occupancy, with BIM processes and models providing the data to support GSL.

The decision points outlined within the BS8536 series should be contained within a Plan of Work which directly relates to key decision points and work stage gateways which determine the progression of a project from Stage 0 – Strategy through to Stage 7 – Operation and End of Life. Each Workstage requires continual review and feedback to ensure that the targets for that stage have been met.

BS 8536 is now also incorporated into the UK BIM Framework. Embedding Government Soft Landings (GSL) and  ‘Golden Thread’ as part of a data centric approach

This highlights the overall approach highlighting the importance of performance reviews and feedback loops with lessons learnt captured and fed into future projects. 

The approach provides a clear line of sight from inception right through to handover and operations, enabling the management and safeguarding of the projects stated outcomes.

Transforming Infrastructure Performance: Roadmap to 2030

The TIP Roadmap to 2030 provides a reform for the delivery of infrastructure. Its purpose is to drive a change in infrastructure performance and its affect on the environment using digital technologies and processes on the road to Net zero carbon

Data will be key to informing decision making, from improved information management in delivery to the creation of digital twins for asset maintenance and optimisation.

Successful delivery requires new collaborative ways of working and embracing of technologies to deliver better performance across the asset lifecycle. 

The new Project Outcome Profile will be a key tool in supporting outcomes relating to the built environment and the provision of services, changing the ways we plan, design, manufacture, construct and operate.
