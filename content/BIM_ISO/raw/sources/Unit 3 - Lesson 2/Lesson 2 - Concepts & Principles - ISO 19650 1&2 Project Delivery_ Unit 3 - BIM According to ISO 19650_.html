BIM according to ISO 19650

A process of delivering right level of information at the correct information exchange point defined, relating to the design, construction, operational and maintenance of a building or asset, using appropriate open technology formats.

A successful project example,  adopting ‘BIM according to the ISO 19650 series’  processes, would have the following features: 

  • Clear information requirements defined by an asset owner or appointing party. 

    bullet

    Clear information requirements defined by an asset owner or appointing party. 

  • Processes in place for the production and review of information.

    bullet

    Processes in place for the production and review of information.

  • An appropriate level of information need, without compromising health and safety or security. 

    bullet

    An appropriate level of information need, without compromising health and safety or security. 

  • Efficient, collaborative exchange of information.

    bullet

    Efficient, collaborative exchange of information.

  • Timely decision making processes.

    bullet

    Timely decision making processes.

The final result would be  efficiencies gained through reduction of waste/rework in the design, construction, operation and maintenance activities plus the reduction of risk.

Purpose of Information

To support decision making, the purpose of information for each stakeholder needs to be considered

Each stakeholder in the project wishes to use the information for a specific set of purposes: for example from a:

  1. From a project delivery perspective it is used to plan and organize the work with supporting information which may come from a BIM execution plan;

  1. From an asset user’s perspective it is used to make sure the asset has the right qualities and supporting information may come from an asset information model

  1. From an asset owner’s perspective it is used to make strategic business decisions with supporting information that may come from a strategic asset portfolio review;

  1. From a Health & Safety perspective the information ma be used to improve site safety, the safety of the public, and to establish and review risks via effective risk management 

Other purposes of information include:

  • Costings

  • Sequencing

  • Operations

  • End of life / decommissioning 

  • Information Security

The Information Management Process

There are four overarching principles relating to the information delivery cycle:

  1. Decision points occur during all parts of an asset life cycle, as part of an asset management system

  2. Information is specified and then delivered progressively through information exchanges at set milestones. 


3. Where a delivery team consists of multiple parties, the information requirements should be passed to the most relevant party

4. Information exchanges should take place through a CDE to defined procedures, in open BIM standards where possible to encourage interoperability.



Project's Information Protocol

An information management process is started each time a new appointment is made.

ISO 19650-2 requires an appointing party to establish the project’s information protocol, and included it within each appointment.

Lead appointed parties must also include the project’s information protocol within all delivery team sub-appointments.

01:09

Appointor & Appointee

The protocol introduces terminology such as Appointor and Appointee, this is a more neutral term than the ISO definitions.

Fundamentally the Appointor is the Appointing Party and the Appointee is the lead appointed party and the appointed parties.

These flexible terms enable the protocol to work alongside any contract or sub-contract at any level.

A key feature of the Information Protocol is that all appointment and project-specific information, processes and documents is in one place, the Information Particulars. 

Due to the legal issues relating to other protocols not fully aligning with ISO, in June 2020, the UK BIM Framework produced an Information protocol to support ISO19650-2,  the delivery phase of assets, and in November 2021, a protocol to support ISO19650-3,  the operational phase.

The UK BIM framework protocol outlines a contractual conduct to adhere to, which deals with risk allocation,, roles, responsibilities, and the legal rights of the parties involved. These protocols are contractually binding, and this protocol even incorporates a new incorporation clause.

Legal & Commercial Implications of ISO 19650

An issue in terms of legal implications with the introduction of ISO 19650 is the new terminology and how this can map across contractually.

There are additional aspects to consider such as: 

  • Intellectual Property & Copyright

  • Liability

  • Model & Data Ownership

  • Collaboration

  • Security of Information

  • Risk Allocation

Most projects use a standard form of contract, and both JCT and NEC4 have recently updated their clauses and provided guidance on how to incorporate BIM.

In 2018 an insightful report was produced by The Winfield Rock Report, discussing standards, contracts, BIM-specific documents and the legal community generally.

The report highlighted a lack of guidance and resources available to people preparing BIM contracts.

00:38

Since the release of the report, in 2019 JCT produced a new practice note, BIM and JCT Contracts providing guidance on construction contracts using BIM.

The aim of BIM and JCT Contracts is to:

  • Further the understanding of BIM related legal and contractual issues and suggest ways of approaching such issues in a collaborative and constructive way

  • Provide practical, clear guidance to project participants and their professional advisers.

BIM and JCT Contracts is structured as follows:

  • Part A - a commentary on BIM and the JCT Design and Build Contract 2016, pointing out provisions that may be most impacted by or relevant to a project using BIM. It provides suggestions on matters to bear in mind, including preparing and incorporating a BIM Protocol.

  • Part B - sets out suggestions on the main topics that may sensibly be covered in a BIM Protocol.

  • Appendix 1 – a checklist of typical items to be considered by the Employer and the Employer’s professional advisers in formulating the Exchange Information Requirements at pre-tender stage.

  • Appendix 2 – Glossary of terms

  • Appendix 3 - The Design and Build Contract clauses referred to in Part A for ease of reference.

  • Bibliography to enable readers to further their knowledge and understanding.

ISO 19650 series deals information requirements associated with project delivery, asset management and also security of information in addition.

There are four key documents which define the appointing party’s information requirements: 

1. Organizational information requirements (OIRs) – In relation to organizational objectives 

2. Project information requirements (PIRs) – In relation to the purpose, design and construction of an asset 

3. Asset information requirements (AIRs) – In relation to the operation and maintenance of an asset 

4. Exchange information requirements (EIRs) – in relation to an appointment 

As you can see, these information requires specify the information deliverables.

In this figure ‘encapsulates’ means ‘provides input to’, ‘contributes to’ means ‘provides an input to’ and ‘specifies’ means ‘determines the content, structure and methodology’.



Non Project Documents: OIR

The Organizational information requirements (OIR)  are produced by the appointing party as one of the initial documents prior to AIR, PIR and EIR, providing a high level starting point.

They consider the information required for in the operation and maintenance of an asset or building, before the delivery aspect. The focus is on the whole lifecycle and how this information can inform the appointing party and the potential building operator.

It is key to Identify the purposes for which information is required.

The purposes could be to satisfy:

• Objectives/outcomes

• Stakeholders 

• Regulations

• Policies 

• Business operations

An example we see often at the BRE is compliance with BREEAM and how this is managed. 

Non Project Documents: AIR

The AIR is formed from the OIR as shown below, developing those high level requirements in relation to the required maintainable assets in the form of an AIR. 

Typically the facilities management will drive the contents of the AIR, defining asset information required in order to undertake the ongoing maintenance of the building or asset.

Information delivery is typically required in an Open BIM format for interoperability with CAFM systems.

Typical examples could be data relating to COBie, classification, warranties, serial codes etc. 

AIR are created by The appointing party, client, the asset owner or third party representative.

AIM & PIM

  • The Asset information model (AIM) supports the facilities and asset management processes established by the appointing party. These models are rich in meta-data as defined by the AIR, typically with a high level of information relating to the object data required for the operational phase of an asset

  • Project information model (PIM) is used during the delivery phase, and will typically contain the required information to satisfy task team deliverables. The PIM may also include scheduling, costing, and health and  safety information. PIM contributes to the AIM

AIM and PIM are produced throughout the information life cycle.

Common Data Environment (CDE)

‘Agreed source of information for any given project or asset, for collecting, managing and disseminating each information container through a managed process’  ISO 19650-1

A CDE workflow describes the processes to be used and a CDE solution can provide the technology.

Single Source of the Truth

Provision of a CDE

The common data environment is an agreed source of information consisting of several states as shown. As an information container develops, it transitions through the states.

The Common Data Environment has four main areas:

  1. WORK IN PROGRESS

  2. SHARED

  3. PUBLISHED

  4. ARCHIVE

This is a process, not a software solution. There is a lot of confusion where organisations talk about buying a CDE as a software solution. There are software solutions that meet certain parts of its requirements but cannot meet all of them.

A common data environment solution and workflow should allow information containers, and the information itself, to be accessed by those who require it when undertaking their function.

So, the states or areas in broad terms are:

This is the state where task teams carry out their work to produce their deliverables. 

For example it could be:

  • The desktop or a laptop

  • A centralised server

  • SharePoint

Deliverables have yet to be approved and should not be shared with other members of the project team as there is a high risk of work being incorrect and uncoordinated. It is the only state where information a task team is responsible for can be revised. These deliverables then need to go through the ‘Check-Review-Approve’ transition process.

The shared state is the ‘single source of truth’ as it is where the information, has been delivered to the deliver team to allow the controlled exchange of information, and must be read-only.


This is where most software solutions focus on the sharing as it is where the information has been provided by the Architects, Engineers etc and has been through a sign off process.

This is used so that only the information that the client needs to see can be shared. For example on a Design and Build project, where a design team is providing options to be priced, and the Main Contractor may not want the client to be aware of these options for political reasons. Therefore there is a separate ‘Approval’ process so the Lead Supplier, Lead Designer etc can sign off information before it transitions to the Client Shared Area. This means there may be a Client’s EDMS for Client shared, a Contractor’s EDMS for Shared.

Following the Review and Authorization Process for information is transitioned to the Published state for a particular use. This use could be Legal, planning, building control, tendering; essentially anything that there is a contractual purpose or requirement to deliver.

The Archived state is used to record the audit trail of information containers that are transitioned to SHARED, CLIENT SHARED or PUBLISHED states.


Anything that transitions through the gateways needs to be archived, so there is a record of who issues what. If we go back to what has driven this pre-Egan, was the Heathrow express collapse, and the problem they had here was that they could not track what information had been issued and who was responsible for what. Standardized archiving was one of the great pushes out of the Hackett Report.
