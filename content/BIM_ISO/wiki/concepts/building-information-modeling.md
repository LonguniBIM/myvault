# Building Information Modeling (BIM)

**BIM** is defined as a combination of process, standards, and technology through which it is possible to generate, visualise, exchange, assure, and subsequently use and re-use information. 

It forms a trustworthy foundation for decision-making for the benefit of all those involved in any part of an asset’s lifecycle. This includes inception, capital phase procurement and delivery, asset and facility management, maintenance, refurbishment, and ultimately an asset’s disposal or re-use.

> As the ISO 19650 series relates to information management using building information modelling, a more appropriate meaning for the BIM acronym may be **Better Information Management**.

## Core Pillars of BIM

BIM is underpinned by four main pillars (plus Data):

### People
- Defined Responsibilities
- Competency
- Software Skills
- Experience

### Process
- Resource Management
- Standard Implementation
- Quality Assurance

### Policy
- Standards
- Risks and insurance
- Procurement
- Legal & Contractual

### Technology
- Hardware
- Software
- Common Data Environment (CDE)

### Data
- Asset Data
- Operational Data
- Data Security
- Exchange Methods
