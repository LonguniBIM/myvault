## A History of BIM


> [!note] Audio transcript (02:31)
> In 1976, BRE published Coordinating Working Drawings. This publication surveyed the effect of poorly coordinated working drawings from 25 different construction projects. In 1987, in an attempt to aid the industry, the Building Project Information Committee, BPIC, was formed under joint sponsorship from a number of industry bodies, such as the RIBA, RICS and CITA in 1987. The committee is now known as Construction Project Information Committee, CPIC. In 1994, the Latham Report was published, which was one of many publications which was quite critical of the UK construction industry. Key issues identified in the Latham Report were issues such as lack of client involvement, as well as poor client satisfaction. A number of negative terms were associated with the industry, including ineffective and adversarial. As such, the report recommended the use of teamwork to improve the industry's performance and achieve better value for money. Rethinking Construction. Four years later, in 1998, the Egan Report was published. This report was to look at the progress UK construction has made since Latham. The results were not positive. There was a deep concern that the industry was underachieving. It suggested that the industry had low profitability and did not invest in research, development and training. The report also recommended further client involvement, noting that projects with client-led initiatives had seen dramatic improvements. Concluding the report, new targets were set, including an annual capital cost reduction of 10%, as well as a reduction in post-construction defects by 20%. Accelerating Change. In 2002, Egan's second report, Accelerating Change, was published. This was a much more positive report than the first, commenting on the need to further improve based on the positive reaction that industry has had since Rethinking Construction. Continuing on from previous reports, Accelerating Change suggests that clients should lead the process and facilitate the creation of integrated teams. The report set out a target that, by the end of 2004, 20% of construction projects by value should be undertaken by integrated teams and supply chains. And, by the end of 2007, both of these figures should rise to 50%.

> [!note] Audio transcript (01:00)
> The ISO-19650 standards relating to the organisation and digitisation information management using Building Information Modelling is divided into five parts. The first of the documents to be released were Parts 1 and 2 in 2018, BSEN ISO-19650-1 2018 Part 1, Concepts and Principles, defines the concepts and principles for processes for defining information requirements and a framework in support of management and production of information during the life cycle of the bill assets. This is referred to as Information Management, BSEN ISO-19650-2, 2018 Part 2, Delivery Phase of the Assets defines additional processes and information exchange principles relating to the planning, management, exchange and delivery of information. These superseded the BIM Level 2 standards and this set of standards became known as BIM according to ISO-19650.

## Ten-Question Analysis Framework

### 1. Problem
What historical inefficiencies, adversarial contract relations, and uncoordinated documentation drove the construction industry to standardize information management?

### 2. Applicability
- **Timeline**: 1976 (BRE Working Drawings) through 1994 (Latham), 1998 (Egan), 2007 (BS 1192), 2011 (UK Government Construction Strategy), to 2018-2022 (ISO 19650 series).

### 3. Process
1. Industry reports expose systemic flaws (Latham: adversarial culture; Egan: lack of investment, high defect rates).
2. Development of technical codes of practice (BS 1192:2007 CDE workflow).
3. Government client mandates (UK 2016 Level 2 mandate) drive market tipping point.
4. Internationalization via ISO 19650 series replacing domestic British Standards.

### 4. Evidence
- Seminal publications: Latham Report (Constructing the Team), Egan Report (Rethinking Construction), BS 1192:2007, PAS 1192-2/3/4/5.

### 5. Principles
- **Client Leadership**: Lasting improvement requires clients/appointing parties to mandate structured information from the outset.
- **Collaborative Production**: Transition from isolated discipline drafting to federated, coordinated environments.

### 6. Risks & Anti-patterns
- Reverting to adversarial, fragmented silo working despite adopting 3D software tools.

### 7. Operationalization
- Standardized supply chain collaboration protocols and integrated project insurance models.

### 8. Skill Test
Can this become a Skill?
No (historical & context analysis).

### 9. Skill Design
- **Supported Role**: Educational / Policy / Industry Leadership.

### 10. Validation Plan
Trace modern ISO 19650 clauses back to the original industry defects identified in Latham and Egan.
