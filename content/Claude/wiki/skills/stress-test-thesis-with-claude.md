# Stress-Test Thesis with Claude

## Why This Matters

AI is naturally sycophantic (too agreeable). If you ask for feedback, it often just nods. This skill forces Claude to be a relentless critic to help you seek truth rather than just validation.

## The Skill

Force a rigorous counter-argument phase followed by a "Discernment Filter" to improve your final output.

## Steps

### 1. The Critique Prompt

1. Paste your draft or thesis into Claude.
2. Use the exact trigger prompt:
   `"Argue against everything in this piece. Be specific. Point to the weakest claims."`
3. Let Claude "demolish" your argument mechanically.

### 2. The Discernment Filter

1. **Step away from the screen** for 1-2 minutes after reading Claude's critique.
2. Ask yourself: `"Do I actually think I was wrong, or did I just get out-argued by something that doesn't care what's true?"`
3. Identify which of Claude's points are structural flaws vs. just "smart-sounding" rhetoric.

### 3. Iterative Refinement

1. Push back on Claude's weak counter-arguments.
2. Accept the valid gaps it identified.
3. Prompt: `"Now rewrite the draft. Incorporate the valid critiques about [Point A] and [Point B], but ignore the critiques about [Point C] because [Your Reason]."`

## Watch Out For

- **Folding/Surrendering**: Assuming the AI is right just because its argument is well-put. Even experts fold.
- **Validation-Seeking**: Don't ask "what do you think?" Ask "why is this wrong?"
- **The "Rush" of Agreement**: If you feel a rush of "oh wow, it's right," be extra skeptical.

## Success Criteria

- [ ] Identified at least 2 non-obvious weak claims.
- [ ] Rejected at least 1 "smart-sounding" but invalid AI critique.
- [ ] Final draft has been refined based on valid gaps.
