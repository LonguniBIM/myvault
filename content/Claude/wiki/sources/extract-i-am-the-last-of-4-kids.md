## Six-Question Analysis

### Q1 — What problem does this solve?

It solves the "AI sycophancy" and "blind agreement" problem. LLMs are trained to be helpful and often nod along even if the user is wrong. This document provides a framework for using AI to stress-test ideas while warning against the trap of "folding" when the AI's counter-arguments sound too convincing.

### Q2 — What numbered steps does the author prescribe?

**The Stress-Test Ritual**:
1. Finish a draft or define a core thesis.
2. Paste it into Claude.
3. Use the specific prompt: **"Argue against everything in this piece. Be specific. Point to the weakest claims."**
4. Review the gaps and weak claims identified.
5. **The Discernment Filter**: Step away from the screen and ask: "Do I actually think I was wrong, or did I just get out-argued by something that doesn't care what's true?"
6. Rewrite the draft by steering the conversation—pushing back on AI flaws while accepting valid structural critiques.

### Q3 — What principles recur?

- **AI Sycophancy**: AI defaults to agreement; you must explicitly force it to be a critic.
- **Being Right vs. Seeking Truth**: The goal isn't to have the AI tell you you're brilliant, but to find where your logic fails.
- **The "Rush" Warning**: A specific feeling of "oh wow, it's right, I was wrong" is often a signal of being out-argued by a machine, not necessarily a discovery of truth.
- **Discernment cannot be taught**: It is a feeling/awareness that must be practiced through active resistance to AI "smart-talk."

### Q4 — What mistakes does the author warn against?

- **Folding/Surrendering**: Assuming that because the AI's counter-argument is well-put, it is automatically true (even experts like Andrej Karpathy fall for this).
- **Chasing Validation**: Using AI as a "yes-man" instead of a sparring partner.
- **Passive Ingestion**: Reading about discernment and thinking you have the skill without practicing it in a high-stakes conversation.

### Q5 — What diagnostic questions does the author pose?

- Am I chasing validation or truth?
- Do I actually think I was wrong, or did I just get out-argued?
- If I told Claude the Earth was flat, would it help me build the case (testing for sycophancy)?
