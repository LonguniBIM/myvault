## Six-Question Analysis

### Q1 — What problem does this solve?

It solves the problem of "AI-obvious" writing. Specifically, it targets "negative parallelism" (the "It's not X, it's Y" pattern) and other machine-like patterns (puffery, metronome rhythm, banned vocabulary) that trigger human detection and kill engagement.

### Q2 — What numbered steps does the author prescribe?

**The Anti-AI Audit Setup**:
1. **Create the File**: Build an `anti-ai-writing-style.md` file.
2. **Define the Banned Patterns**:
   - **Hard Ban**: Negative parallelism (any reframe where you reject X to replace with Y).
   - **Vocabulary**: Ban 100+ "AI-words" (delve, realm, harness, unlock, leverage, etc.).
   - **Phrase Shapes**: Avoid bloated verbs ("serves as," "marks a")—use "is" or "has."
   - **Pacing**: Force short paragraphs (1-2 sentences) and varied rhythm.
3. **Deployment**:
   - Save this file in your root `Claude Cowork` folder.
   - Update **Global Instructions** to force Claude to read this file before every task.
4. **The Audit Loop**:
   - After Claude generates text, use the prompt: **"Audit it against the anti-ai-writing-style.md file."**
   - Remind Claude if it misses any patterns.
5. **Continuous Maintenance**: Every 3 months, review last 10 drafts, circle robotic patterns, and add them to the banned list.

### Q3 — What principles recur?

- **Spirit over Letter**: Rules are for guidance; clean, natural writing wins over rigid adherence.
- **Directness is Human**: Avoid fake depth and "assistant performance."
- **Patterns are giveaways**: "Negative parallelism" is the #1 tell of 2026 AI writing.
- **Living Document**: AI patterns drift; your banned list must evolve to stay ahead of "AI noise."

### Q4 — What mistakes does the author warn against?

- **Throat-clearing**: AI tends to start with a summary of what it's *about* to do. Delete it.
- **Puffery/Inflation**: Using "seismic shift" for minor facts. Let the reader judge the weight.
- **Rule of Three**: Defaulting to lists of 3 items just to sound thorough.
- **Elegant Variation**: Swapping "Claude" for "the model" to avoid repetition. Just repeat the name.

### Q5 — What diagnostic questions does the author pose?

- Does this sound useful, or overworked?
- Does this sentence pivot from a rejected frame (X) to a revealed one (Y)?
- If this style rule makes the sentence awkward, am I prioritizing accuracy/clarity?
