## BIM according to ISO 19650

A process of delivering right level of information at the correct information exchange point defined, relating to the design, construction, operational and maintenance of a building or asset, using appropriate open technology formats.

![A high level example of information delivery can be seen in this flowchart based on ISO 19650-1 Figure 4,encapsulating information requirements, planning, delivery, approval and also feedback loops.](assets/PxOFcQ6rd3BjDAtn_n_bJylrcTivN_1DJ.png)

## Project's Information Protocol


An information management process is started each time a new appointment is made.

ISO 19650-2 requires an appointing party to establish the project’s information protocol, and included it within each appointment.

Lead appointed parties must also include the project’s information protocol within all delivery team sub-appointments.

> [!note] Audio transcript (01:09)
> An information management process is started each time a new appointment is made. ISO19650-2 requires an appointing party to establish the project's information protocol and include it within each appointment as part of the contract. This document is not standalone. Lead appointed parties must also include the project's information protocol within all delivery team sub-appointments. The protocol is a document required by ISO19650, which agrees the rights and responsibilities of the parties to comply with ISO19650-2. The CIC BIM protocol, second edition, was typically used as the starting point in the UK as part of the previous BIM Level 2 standards. However, there are issues in terms of the transition from BIM Level 2 to ISO19650 not being a direct like for like, raising legal implications. The terminology changes, process requirements and legal liabilities are not reflected and therefore it is not fully adapted to be used on ISO19650 as yet. The second edition was released in April 2018, so only aligned to the PAS 1992 suite.

## Non Project Documents: OIR

The Organizational information requirements (OIR)  are produced by the appointing party as one of the initial documents prior to AIR, PIR and EIR, providing a high level starting point.

They consider the information required for in the operation and maintenance of an asset or building, before the delivery aspect. The focus is on the whole lifecycle and how this information can inform the appointing party and the potential building operator.

It is key to Identify the purposes for which information is required.

The purposes could be to satisfy:

**• Objectives/outcomes**

**• Stakeholders **

**• Regulations**

**• Policies **

**• Business operations**

An example we see often at the BRE is compliance with BREEAM and how this is managed.

![m3s207.PNG](assets/LyHWa3L2LtV-ryR9_LXoyjhWnbwNT4nhF.png)

## Non Project Documents: AIR

The AIR is formed from the OIR as shown below, developing those high level requirements in relation to the required maintainable assets in the form of an AIR.

Typically the facilities management will drive the contents of the AIR, defining asset information required in order to undertake the ongoing maintenance of the building or asset.

Information delivery is typically required in an Open BIM format for interoperability with CAFM systems.

Typical examples could be data relating to COBie, classification, warranties, serial codes etc.

AIR are created by The appointing party, client, the asset owner or third party representative.

## AIM & PIM


- The Asset information model (AIM) supports the facilities and asset management processes established by the appointing party. These models are rich in meta-data as defined by the AIR, typically with a high level of information relating to the object data required for the operational phase of an asset
- Project information model (PIM) is used during the delivery phase, and will typically contain the required information to satisfy task team deliverables. The PIM may also include scheduling, costing, and health and  safety information. PIM contributes to the AIM
AIM and PIM are produced throughout the information life cycle.
